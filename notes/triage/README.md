# BB(6) holdout triage (November 2026)

Which of the open BB(6) holdouts are most likely to be provable, or to halt, and are not being proved by someone
else? This is a ranking for choosing what to work on next.

**Everything here is simulated or read from other people's notes. Nothing is proved.** A warning sign is a
heuristic; a score is a sorting key, not a probability; "no halt" means no halt up to the step reached.

Short answer:

* 608 holdouts (mxdys, 30 Oct 2026) − 85 in open/unmerged busycoq PRs − 1 proved since − 2 already studied =
  **520 candidates**. All 520 were run in the fast pass. **None halted.**
* 336 of the 520 carry at least one fatal sign (lottery, Collatz-like, no structure, cryptid), from simulation or
  notes. 46 carry no sign at all; most of those are machines with little written about them, not clean ones.
* The top 10 by score, after about 10 to 20 minutes of deeper simulation each, sort into three groups (section
  "Top 10"):
  * **Clean, with the hard part of 13/#9 not seen:** 65980 (timer + registers, 13 runs, 7 shapes).
  * **Clean, but with 13's hard part (a band or timers worn down at growing scales):** 19219 (+44531, 157488),
    23154 (+115779), 97939 (+ its twin 99198), 144438. Here the tools and lemma style from 13 carry over directly,
    and the obstacle is the same one 13 has.
  * **Harder than the score says:** 112769 (+152969) (phase map with multiplier 8/3 and a 3-adic condition:
    Collatz-like risk), 40361 (a race decided about 10^12 rounds out, already reduced in Lean and actively worked
    on elsewhere), 5380 (+173888) and 78071 (+111043) (tens of thousands of irregular domains: no compact
    structure at large scale), 149064 (binary counter of many parts, not decoded).
* New simulation result: **19219 does not halt in its first 4.8·10^20 steps** (20 min of `tri.c`, exact). The
  explorer's run stopped at 1.0·10^19. The overflow near 3.3·10^19 used up two far blocks (644 and 494 words)
  without halting (top 10, rank 8; `deep/19219.long.txt`).
* **Likely to halt** (from notes, not from this simulation): the probviously-halting cryptids (Lucy's Moonlight
  166535, 8084, 103424) and the Reus vampire family (82614, 121010, 134470, 92948; five of its members were
  simulated to a halt by Ahmer Raza in October). None is cheap: each halts, if at all, far beyond direct simulation.

## Method

Everything here is **simulation and reading of past notes**. Nothing is proved, and every warning sign below is a
heuristic. The scores rank the machines for a first look. They do not measure difficulty.

### 1. The list (stage 1, `STAGE1.md`)

* mxdys's 608-machine list (30 Oct 2026, `data/BB6_holdouts_608.txt`), the newest on the wiki's *Holdouts lists* page.
* Dropped: **85** machines in a pull request of ccz181078/busycoq that is not merged yet (#43 to #102, plus #3, #4
  and #40, found from the `refs/pull/N/head|merge` refs; `data/pr_machines.txt`); **1** machine proved in busycoq's
  BB6 branch after the list (FIBv7); and the **2** machines already studied (13 and #9). That leaves **520**.
* Machines are matched across sources by a canonical form: L/R mirror plus state renaming (`tools/canon.py`).
  Matching misses equivalences other than these (for example, the same run from a different table). The
  busy-beaver-explorer `related.json` covers some of them and is reported per machine.

### 2. Past research (all read as data)

* **Spreadsheet** (community tracker): class and status for all 608 (`sheet` in `data/sources.json`).
* **busy-beaver-explorer** (`holdouts/*/status.json`, `external.json`, `groups.json`, `related.json`, `known.json`):
  a hand-written verdict for about 600 machines, a count of claims proved in Lean (kernel), families, related
  tables, and wiki labels.
* **AI analysis artifact** (claude.ai/artifact/8B8E5ms18otN4XHbEnxaP2): per-machine status (`pattern`, `rules`,
  `lottery`, `cryptid`, `little`, `reported`, `pr`, `ready`), a one-line note, and the farthest simulated step.
* **busycoq**: merged proofs (BB6 branch, last merge 3 Nov 2026) and open PRs (who is solving which families:
  Alex Havrilla, Alk, Dennis Koshta, valu42, Claude-authored PRs, Yeyito, Thomas Reus).
* **bbchallenge wiki**: *BB(6)* (cryptid and potential-cryptid lists), *Cryptids*, *Collatz-like*, *Shift overflow
  counter*, *TMBR: October/November 2026*, and the wiki pages of individual machines through `known.json`.

**Existing triage or classification, and overlap.** The wiki search finds no page on triage. Earlier
classifications exist: the spreadsheet's class column (Shift-overflow counter, Bell-like, Skelet 1-like, Chaotic
Bell, Spaghetti, ...); mxdys's *BB6_holdouts_classified_1343* list (Dec 2025, on the wiki's Holdouts lists page);
busy-beaver-explorer's growth-shape triage (`exp`/`sqrt`/`power`/`linear`/`fixed` per tape side, 10^9 steps) and
families; the AI artifact's status labels; and dyuan's subset of 24 machines expected to yield to longitudinal
analysis (TMBR October 2026, a Discord post not readable here). This triage reuses those labels as one input
(the "notes" signs) and adds its own simulation signs. Where they agree, the penalty is larger.

### 3. Fast pass (stage 2, `tools/fastpass.py`, `tools/tri.c`)

* `tri.c` generalizes `chainb.c` (branch `bb6-1RB---_1LC0LC_1RD1LB_1LE1RD_0LA1LF_1LD0RF`) to any 6-state machine
  and to a block size K = 1..8 chosen at run time. The tape is a list of runs of K-cell blocks with one carried
  block behind the head. A whole run is crossed in one macro step when one block returns the same state, direction
  and carried block.
* **Exactness check** (`tools/exact_check.sh`): for 25 random candidates, every K and two step budgets, the full
  final configuration (state, head, tape) equals that of a plain one-cell simulator after the same number of
  steps: 400 of 400 identical.
* Per machine: probe K = 1..8 for 0.5 s each and keep the K with the most tape cells per run (within 10% of the
  best, the one that went furthest), then run 15 s with a log of tape statistics at steps 2^(i/4) and of the tape
  at returns to either end of the visited region (`data/fastpass.jsonl`; logs are not committed).
* 15 s of a block simulator usually reaches 10^10 to 10^17 steps. That is often **less** than the community's
  simulations (most holdouts are run to 10^13 or more). The pass is for structure signs, not for new halts.

### 4. Warning signs (`tools/analyze.py`: simulation; `tools/score.py`: notes)

| sign | kind | simulation proxy | notes proxy |
|---|---|---|---|
| lottery | **fatal** | walls with irregular material between them (12+ distinct short blocks, runs/blocks 0.15 to 0.5, growing) | AI status `lottery`; "pseudo-random", "random walk" |
| Collatz-like | **fatal** | the largest run is rebuilt 4+ times and its peaks grow by a near-constant non-integer factor | "Collatz", "3x/2", "4/3 map" |
| no structure | **fatal** | runs per block > 0.5 at every K, and runs grow with the tape | Spaghetti, Chaotic Bell/Fractal/1dCA classes |
| cryptid | **fatal** | (none) | wiki or spreadsheet cryptid / potential cryptid; AI status `cryptid` |
| drift | manageable | at returns to the same end in the same state, the non-wall part changes slowly and monotonically while the tape grows | "drift", "used up", "slowly" |
| overflows | manageable | 2+ big rearrangements (largest run or run count halves) in the last half of the log-scale run | "overflow" in the prose (not the class name) |
| late changes | manageable | 2+ block types in the big runs at the end not seen in big runs before 30% of the run | (none) |
| several parts | manageable | the three largest runs, at two returns to the same point a decade apart, change size ratios by > e^0.5 | "two counters", "registers", "two-stack", "nested" |
| rare exceptions | manageable | returns to an end in one state, except < 3% | "rare", "exception" |

The wiki adds two kinds of hardness that the table covers only through notes: **cryptids**, whose halting is a
known hard problem (Collatz-like or random-walk), and **probviously halting** machines, which will probably halt
but too far out to simulate (handled in "likely to halt" below).

### 5. Score (`tools/score.py`)

Start at 50. Each fatal kind: -22 (-28 when simulation and notes agree). Each manageable kind: -5. Compressible tape
in simulation (runs per block < 0.2): +8. Rules proved in Rocq or Lean (notes): +10. An exact model or law
(notes): +6. A reduction of halting to one precise statement (notes): +6. Worked on in a dedicated
busy-beaver-explorer folder: -10. A related table in an open PR: -3. Resembles a studied machine with the hard
part absent: +10 (section 6). Ties and near-ties mean nothing.

### 6. Comparison with the studied machines (`tools/compare.py`)

* **13-like** (a counter with walls and digit words, `1RB---_1LC0LC_1RD1LB_1LE1RD_0LA1LF_1LD0RF`): at the dominant
  return point, 3+ large runs with a near-constant size ratio >= 1.8 and logarithmic growth; or notes describing a
  base-N counter, an odometer, digit words or nested counters. The hard part of 13 is the band slowly used up and
  overflows at growing scales. It counts as absent when neither the drift nor the overflow sign is seen.
* **#9-like** (a phase/timer structure with a counter word, `1RB0LF_1RC1RB_0RD0RC_1LE1LF_1LD---_0LB1LA`): the largest
  run counts down step by step between returns and is reset at phase starts to growing values; or notes describing
  a timer, countdown or sync bouncer counter. The hard part of #9 is the lottery. It counts as absent when no lottery
  sign is seen.

## Top 10

from busy-beaver-explorer's `related.json`) folded into one entry, because one proof method would serve all
of them. That takes the list to rank 13 (23154 and 65980 come in at score 73).

Deep dive per machine: a 120 s run of `tri.c` at the fast pass's block size (10^15 to 2·10^19 steps), every
return to the dominant end logged after the fast pass's last step (`tools/deep.py`). Then a drift test: at that
return point, which runs counted from the far end of the tape ever change, and how (`tools/deep2.py`). Plus the
past notes. "Drift test passed" would mean every quantity except the growing counters returns to exactly the same
value at the same point of each cycle. **No machine in the top 10 passes it outright.** The far parts are either
frozen between rare events and changed by them, or worn down slowly.

## Comparison with the two studied machines (3b)

Flags come from `tools/compare.py` (simulation and notes); the deep dives above override them for the top 10.

* **Flagged 13-like** (counter with walls and digit words): 98 machines, mostly because their notes name a base-N
  counter, odometer or digit words, and 14 with simulated geometric walls. 13's hard part was judged absent for 64
  (13-like or #9-like) in the fast pass. **The deep dives contradict this for every 13-like machine in the top 10:**
  97939, 144438, 19219 and 23154 all have a band or timers worn down at growing scales, and 40361 has its own race.
  The fast-pass drift detector looked only at the material between walls. It missed wear on the far walls, which
  changes only at rare overflows. So the "hard part absent" flags for 13-like machines outside the top 10 should be
  treated as unverified.
* **Flagged #9-like** (phase/timer with a counter word): 36 machines. In the top 10: **112769** (timer
  confirmed; its risk is Collatz-like rather than a lottery), **65980** (timer confirmed; no lottery seen) and
  **19219** (timer plus band).
* **Rank higher because the hard part is absent:** of the top 10 only 65980 qualifies after the deep dive. 19219
  and 23154 have 13's hard part, but in a cleaner form than 13 (fewer, frozen far blocks; a slower band), so 13's
  `chainb.c`-style tools, end-region tracking and lemma generation apply directly.

## Likely to halt

No machine halted in this simulation (fast pass: 10^10 to 10^17 steps per machine; deep runs: 10^15 to 2·10^19, and
4.8·10^20 for 19219).
From the notes:

* probviously halting cryptids: 166535 (Lucy's Moonlight), 8084 ("potentially probviously halting, exponential
  runtime"), 103424, and the 16-machine family around 24009 (wiki: probviously halting);
* the Reus vampire family: 82614, 121010, 134470, 92948 ("a tiny chance of halting per Bell"); members of the
  family were simulated to a halt in October 2026;
* 40361: halts exactly if one charged block runs out before its neighbour; current estimates say the neighbour
  goes first (not likely to halt, but a concrete halting route).

## Caveats

* The fast pass ran 15 s per machine, often fewer steps than the community has simulated. Its signs are proxies
  and miss things; the deep dives found drift that the fast pass did not flag.
* Note signs are keyword matches on other people's prose (AI artifact, busy-beaver-explorer, spreadsheet). They
  inherit those authors' errors, and the AI artifact's claims of Rocq proofs could not be traced to files.
* Open PRs were identified from git refs (`refs/pull/N/merge`) because the GitHub PR page could not be read here.
  PRs #98, #99 and #101 have no merge ref but were dropped anyway (conservative).
* Equivalence matching uses mirror and renaming only.
